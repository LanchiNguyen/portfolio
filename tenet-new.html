Tenet — one deliberate moment

Tenet lets active traders write rules after the market closes. At order-send, it explains which rule applies and what each choice does next. Most interventions pause for a decision; a separate wind-down hard limit stops new 0DTE orders at 3:55.

THE NEXT ORDER
CAN BECOME A REFLEX

Retail apps create momentum with one-tap re-entry. Desktop workstations do it with hotkeys and dense order tickets. Tenet tests a simple idea: before an order that breaks a rule goes out, show the trader the rule they wrote when the market was closed.

SCOPE &
ETHICAL BOUNDARY

Control at send: loss cooldown and size cap can be overridden before waiting; pacing offers wait or cancel. The wind-down preset demonstrates a user-set hard limit on new 0DTE orders at 3:55. Editing that preset and managing existing positions are outside the prototype. Nothing sends automatically.

  • What is simulated: Dart and Meridian are fictional; quotes, fills, and history are simulated and labeled. Broker integration and the import/analysis pipeline remain future work
  • What Tenet is not: a broker, a trading journal, a signal generator, financial advice, or a promise of profitability. One narrow job: restore a single reflective moment at send

ONE JOURNEY,
THREE SURFACES

Identify a pattern → author a rule → trigger it at send → resolve the order → review the Record. The walkthrough below follows the illustrative revenge-sizing pattern through the existing loss-cooldown and size-cap rules. Companion owns the rule; Dart or Meridian checks the order; the Record shows the decision.

Stricter → immediate.
Tightening syncs to both hosts right away; you can raise your bar mid-day.
Looser → next open.
Loosening arms at the next open, so a rule can't be talked down in the moment it exists to interrupt.
Failure → fail open.
If the connection drops, trading proceeds; the gap is recorded in plain view.

4 · RESOLVE,
THEN REVIEW THE QUOTE

Choose the combined reduce-and-wait action, or override before starting. During the cooldown, the order is held and can be cancelled. When the timer ends, the quote refreshes and the trader must review and confirm again. The timer never sends an order; every fill shown here is simulated.

5 · REVIEW
THE RECORD

After confirming or cancelling in Dart, open the Companion Record. The new event carries the host, rule, size, and decision; an override also carries the selected reason. Open it to inspect the entry or add a note. In the side-by-side prototype, that event appears without re-entering it.

Home summarizes the illustrative week as counts: 7× stepped in · 6× waited or trimmed · 1× continued past. Dollar figures sit below with EST or ACTUAL labels and their method. These simulated results are not evidence of Tenet’s effectiveness.

THREE WORDING AND
BEHAVIOR CALLS

A · Name the kind of limit.
Loss cooldown and size cap can be overridden before waiting. Pacing offers wait or cancel. The wind-down preset is different: at 3:55 it stops new 0DTE orders. Calling that a hard limit makes the consequence explicit; it never means automatically closing a position.
B · Make the hierarchy honest.
Actions that follow the rule are emphasized intentionally. Override and cancel remain distinct, visible exits; they do not carry equal visual weight. This aims to make the authored rule easier to follow, but its effect on decisions is untested.
C · Counts before dollars.
Tenet cannot prove it “saved” money. The Record leads with what the rule did. Dollar figures stay secondary and are labeled EST until settled, with fees and slippage excluded.

TRUST, FAILURE &
ACCESSIBILITY

A tool that interrupts trades has to explain its access, its failures, and its limits.

  • Trust & data. Tenet reads order tickets at send and fills after the close, conceptually through an embedded broker SDK. It never reads the screen or other accounts. In the proposed model, disconnecting stops rules from arming and deleting the account removes rules and Record
  • Failure behavior. Failed sync surfaces an explicit retry. Connection loss fails open: trading proceeds and the gap shows in the Record (“feed lost 17 min · trading unaffected · logged”). Events are immutable; notes and error annotations attach alongside
  • Accessibility, as implemented in the prototypes: every intervention path is keyboard-complete; option groups use real radio semantics with arrow keys; status changes are announced live; charts carry text alternatives; reduced motion is respected. No WCAG certification is claimed

RETROSPECTIVE
DESIGN RATIONALE

This retrospective reconstructs the design rationale behind the prototype. These are comparisons of concept directions, not documented user findings or a verified chronology of iterations:

  • Broad journal → one job. Import, session analysis, and annotation would expand the product into a journal. The current prototype concentrates on intervention at send; those broader capabilities remain future work
  • Unclear control → explicit states. The current pause flow separates hold, choice, wait, review, and confirmation. A hard wind-down limit is labeled separately. Nothing sends automatically
  • Outcome claims → behavior in the Record. The current Record logs prototype interactions and labels estimates and simulated fills. It describes what happened in the demo without treating a market outcome as proof the intervention worked

WHAT STILL NEEDS
TESTING

This is a working prototype, not a validated product. The biggest open questions are:

  • No moderated study yet; nothing here is validated with users
  • The broker SDK and import pipeline are conceptual; feasibility is argued, not proven
  • Market outcomes cannot validate behavioral effectiveness; a “saved” trade may have been a missed winner
  • Free-text override reasons and deeper evidence-to-fill linkage remain deferred

Planned first study: moderated think-aloud sessions on the working prototypes with 6–8 active options/futures traders, including prop-evaluation users. It would test comprehension of combined rules, the difference between a pause and a hard limit, visibility of override and cancel, estimate versus actual, stricter-immediate versus looser-at-open, and whether the action hierarchy could inadvertently encourage more trading. A sample this size supports comprehension and tone findings, not efficacy claims.

WHERE IT LANDED

Tenet connects a pattern, an authored rule, an explicit decision at send, and a Record the trader can inspect afterward. Pauses and hard limits have different consequences. Nothing sends on its own.
