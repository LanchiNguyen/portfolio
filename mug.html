Mug — rewards for bringing your own cup

A concept app that rewards skipping the single-use cup at local cafés. No branded bottle to buy; any mug counts. Four of us, three months: survey, flows, brand, three animated prototypes.

OVERVIEW

We cared about this one: less cup waste, and local cafés competing with the chains' loyalty apps. People already know waste is bad; our bet was to make the sustainable choice the convenient one.

RESEARCH

Forgetting and hurry are practical barriers; "see no reason to" is motivational. Reminders and speed fix the practical half. Rewards give the indifferent 21% a reason that isn't ideology.

“How might we make being sustainable the convenient choice, not the extra chore?” THE HMW THAT GUIDED THE DESIGN
PERSONA

From the survey I developed our persona, Sydney: a student grabbing coffee most mornings, usually in a hurry, fluent in apps like Starbucks'. Time and money motivate her first, sustainability second. Every trade-off defers to her convenience.

JOURNEY

The journey map tracks the habit change and marks where an app can step in: reminders when resolve is fresh, progress tracking when it fades, rewards at the register.

IDEATION

A competitive scan showed the gap: everyone rewarded buying; nobody rewarded bringing. We mapped flows and wireframes around six features:

  • Accounts tracking every cup saved, over time and per café
  • A feed where friends see and nudge each other's savings
  • Saved payment for faster ordering and instant discounts
  • Custom avatars: a mug made yours
  • A barcode front and center
  • Local café recommendations, spreading traffic beyond the chains

BRAND

The brand identity was mine end to end. Greens carry the sustainability story; red is saved for the two moments that pay the user, rewards and the barcode. Recoleta Bold for headlines (friendly, retro, café-chalkboard warmth), Montserrat for body, Montserrat Alternates for captions.

QUESTIONS

What a launch would need answered that a semester didn't:

  • Verification. "Any mug counts" is the pitch and the hole. We designed the customer side only.
  • The café economics. We claimed cafés benefit but never interviewed one. The discount has to come from somewhere.
  • Hi-fi validation. The final design was never tested; its green-on-white text deserves a contrast pass.

RETROSPECTIVE The verification mechanic I'd design first:

The customer's barcode scan already tells the register who is paying. Verification only has to answer did they bring a mug, a yes/no the barista knows on sight. The scan fires the discount as "pending"; one barista-side tap (or an existing modifier key) confirms it. No new hardware, and a wrong tap costs one small discount: cheap enough that speed beats policing. Multi-register cafés and rush-hour honesty drift stay unsolved; both are pilot-testable.

SIMULATED The café-side study we should have run. No real baristas participated; profiles and quotes are illustrative:

Objective: would independent cafés fund a bring-your-own-mug discount, and can verification survive a rush? Method: 15-minute intercepts with 5 staff across 3 independent cafés (2 owners, 2 baristas, 1 shift lead), off-peak. Limitation: intercepts surface objections, not adoption behavior.
