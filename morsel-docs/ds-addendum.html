Component notes

The pieces the visual menu is built from, with the rules each one carries. Palette, type and spacing continue from the earlier exploration: cream paper, terracotta accent, Lora for display, Hanken Grotesk for UI, a 4pt grid.

Restaurant card

A strip of up to three dish photos, then name, neighborhood and distance, then a coverage tag: "8 of 12 photographed", "4 photographed · partial menu" or "No photos yet". The whole card is one button; a card with no photos is not tappable and says why. Coverage is a count, never a percentage, so a small menu does not look better covered than it is. On Home the cards sit in a horizontal rail; in the picker they stack as rows with the same strip.

Tab bar

Nearby, Menu, Compare, each with a visible label. A dark pill over reserved space at the bottom of every screen but the dish page, so content never sits under it. Menu opens the last restaurant, or the picker when none has been chosen. Compare carries a badge with the shortlist count, so no separate bar is needed above the menu.

Menu row

  • Thumbnail 104×104 with a short source tag: "Restaurant", "Diner", or "3 photos". Or the no-photo block at the same size: dashed border, camera-off mark, "No photo yet".
  • Name, description (up to three lines), price. The description is the menu's own line, never a review.
  • Compare toggle at the top right, a separate button so the row itself stays a single open action. aria-pressed reflects the shortlist; when the shortlist is full the toggle stays enabled and explains.
  • The row's accessible name reads name, price and the photo note, so a screen reader hears "No photo yet" where a sighted person sees the block.

Source tag

Two forms. Long on the photo itself: "Restaurant photo", "Diner photo". Short on thumbnails: "Restaurant", "Diner". With several photos the tag becomes a count and the dish page shows each source in turn. The tag is glass over the photo, never a color alone: the word carries the meaning.

Photo strip

One photo at a time, 320pt tall, with previous and next buttons as glass pills, a dot indicator and "2 of 3". Swipe also moves. The source label sits bottom left on every frame. The Back button floats over the top left, the same as on a dish without photos, where a 220pt no-photo block takes the photo's place.

Facts

  • Photos: what exists and where from, then one line: appearance can vary between visits. A photo helps picture the food; it does not confirm ingredients or today's presentation.
  • Ingredients: "The menu lists gluten, shellfish." or "Ingredients aren't listed on the menu." Missing information stays visibly missing, and the tracked set is named so a short list is not read as a complete one.

Compare

  • Count: the Compare tab carries a badge once anything is shortlisted; the toggle's toast says "added · Compare 2 of 3".
  • Column: photo or no-photo block, name, restaurant, price, the full description, the ingredients line, Remove. One column per dish, one to three, all equal width. At text sizes of 130% and above, a three-dish shortlist uses two columns, with the third dish on a second row. One "Add another from the menu" button below while there is room; with nothing shortlisted, the screen says so and offers "Go to the menu".
  • Never a cart. No totals, no quantities, no next step past the menu. The subtitle is only the count: "2 of 3 dishes".

Accessibility notes

  • Every action is a real button with a visible focus ring; rows, toggles and photo navigation work from the keyboard.
  • Back returns focus to the row that opened the dish.
  • Text scales to 140% through one custom property; layouts reflow rather than clip, and the compare grid drops from three columns to two.
  • Still to do: a screen-reader pass, contrast measurement on glass tags over photos, and touch-target checks on a device.
