Flow and assumptions

One journey: you are at a restaurant with the menu in front of you. Pick the place, read its menu with photos where they exist, look closer at a dish, compare two or three, decide. built marks what the prototype does. to test marks an assumption the validation plan is written to check.

Home: Nearby built

Restaurant cards with a photo strip and a coverage tag, then a grid of photographed dishes near the fixed area, nearest first. "I'm at a restaurant" sits at the top, one tap away.

→ Picker · Menu · Dish

Restaurant picker built

"Which restaurant are you at?" Photo cards with a coverage tag, filtered as you type. No location request. Places without photos are listed below, plainly labeled and not tappable.

→ Menu

Menu built

The restaurant's sections in its order. Each row: thumbnail or "No photo yet", name, description, price, an add-to-compare toggle. Section jump chips and a coverage line for a full menu; a "partial menu" note for a restaurant that only has its photographed dishes.

→ Dish · Photos view

Photos view built

Only photographed dishes, as a grid, with the count of dishes it leaves out and a link back to the full menu.

→ Dish · Menu

Dish built

Photos one at a time with a source label on each (Restaurant photo, Diner photo) and a counter. Then the menu's description, price, an ingredients line and a one-line note that appearance can vary between visits: a photo helps picture the food but does not confirm ingredients or today's presentation.

→ Add to compare · View the full menu · Back

Compare built

One to three dishes side by side, from one restaurant or several: photo or "No photo yet", name, restaurant, price, description, ingredients line, remove; one column per dish. One "add another" button below while there is room. Reached from the tab bar, which carries the count. Nothing is ordered.

→ Dish · Menu

Back and memory

  • Back from a dish returns to the screen that opened it, Home or the menu, at the same scroll position, with keyboard focus on the card or row that was opened.
  • The labeled tab bar (Nearby, Menu, Compare) sits over reserved space on every screen but the dish page, so content never scrolls under it. Menu opens the last restaurant, or the picker if there is none.
  • The shortlist, the chosen restaurant and the current screen survive a reload. Corrupt or stale saved state falls back to Home.
  • Text size up to 140% reflows every screen without truncation; a three-dish compare uses two columns at 130% and above. A full screen-reader, contrast and touch review is still to do.
