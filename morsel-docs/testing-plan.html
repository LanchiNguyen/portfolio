Usability testing plan

Two moderated rounds on the v3.1 prototype (phone-sized browser window or device). Round 1 pressure-tests the general discovery assumptions on the wireflow — info scent, trust comprehension, recovery. Round 2 is a dedicated safety round on the dietary model, with participants who live with food allergies and a domain reviewer. Neither round has been run — all findings tables are intentionally empty.

Round 1 — general discovery

Participants — 5 sessions, ~30 min each

  • 5 participants who order out or eat out ≥2× a week and decide from photos (screener: "show me the last food photo that made you order something"). The "urban, 22–35" framing is a recruitment hypothesis, not an established segment.
  • Mix: ≥2 who primarily use delivery apps, ≥2 who primarily walk in.
  • Exclude: food-industry professionals, UX designers.
  • Device: their own phone if possible (mirrors real grip + text size), else provided device.

Moderator script

"Thanks for making time. This is an early prototype of a food-discovery app I designed — some data is fake and some buttons are intentionally limited; none of that reflects on you. I'll ask you to do a few things and think out loud. There are no wrong answers: if something is confusing, that's the design failing, not you. You won't hurt my feelings — blunt is the most useful thing you can be."

  • Warm-up (3 min): "Walk me through how you picked the last place you ate that wasn't routine."
  • Core tasks 1–6 below, in order. Task 6 has two variants; alternate them across sessions.
  • Wrap (3 min): "If this existed tomorrow, when would you actually open it? What would make you delete it?"

Round 2 — dedicated safety round

Participants — 4 sessions + 1 domain review

  • 4 participants who manage a real food allergy — their own or a household member's. Recruited separately from Round 1; the allergy model must be tested by the people it exists for, not simulated by proxies.
  • 1 domain review session with a food-allergy professional (registered dietitian, allergy nurse/educator, or a food-service allergen-compliance manager): walk the full allergy path and the copy, and ask where the design overstates what it can promise.
  • Same moderator script as Round 1, plus: "This app can flag allergens in its data, but it can never guarantee a kitchen is safe — I want to know if the design makes that clear or blurs it."

Severity scale

  • S1 — safety/trust breach: user misunderstands the allergy promise or the trust metric in a way that could cause harm or distrust. Fix before any release.
  • S2 — task blocker: cannot complete a core task (choose, save, order handoff) without moderator help.
  • S3 — friction: completes with detours, misclicks, or visible hesitation.
  • S4 — polish: cosmetic or preference-level.

Before / after template

For each S1–S2 finding that changes the design, produce one before/after pair for the case study:

  1. Before: screenshot of the v3.1 state + one-line finding ("4/5 read 90% as a satisfaction score").
  2. After: screenshot of the revised state + what changed and why it addresses the evidence.
  3. Verdict line: how you'll know it worked (re-test criterion or metric).

Keep pairs at identical crop/zoom so the change reads instantly (template: two phone frames side by side, finding above, response below).
