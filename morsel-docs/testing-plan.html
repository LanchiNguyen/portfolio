Validation plan

Three rounds, in order, none of them run yet. Round A asks whether the frustration behind Morsel is shared before anyone reacts to screens. Round B puts the prototype on a table. Round C looks only at photos and trust. The sessions and the decision rules for each are at the end.

Round A · Reading a menu, wanting to see the dish

5–6 conversations, 30 minutes each

  • Tell me about the last time you sat down with a menu and weren't sure what to order. What did you do?
  • Did you look for photos? Where? What did you find, and was it the dish you were reading about?
  • When you did see a photo, what did it settle for you, and what did it not?
  • How did you decide in the end?

Looking for: whether people look for dish photos at the table at all, what they use instead, and what a photo is expected to answer. If nobody looks, the direction changes before any usability work.

Round B · The prototype at a table

6–8 sessions with a printed Elder & Ash menu beside the phone

Neutral tasks; the facilitator never names a screen or a feature.

  1. "You're at this restaurant. Find it and open its menu." (from Home, which shows nearby photos first)
  2. "You want a main for under $25. Pick one you would order."
  3. "You're torn between two. Decide, and tell me what settled it."
  4. "What do you make of this one?" (a dish with no photo)
  5. "Where did that photo come from?" (a dish with three photos)

Measures: task completion without prompting; whether "I'm at a restaurant" or a restaurant card is used to get in; whether the section chips or scrolling are used; whether compare is used at all and with how many dishes; what people say a "No photo yet" dish is; whether the source label is noticed unprompted.

Round C · Photos and trust

Short sessions, 15 minutes, separate from Round B

  • Show the same dish with a restaurant photo, then a diner photo. Which would you rather see first? Why?
  • Ask what a photo tells you about portion size and ingredients. Then show the photo note and ask again.
  • Ask what they would still check with the server.

Looking for: whether the source label changes trust, whether photos are read as proof of portion or ingredients, and whether the note is read at all.

Decision rules, set before running

  • Direction. If fewer than three of six people in Round A describe looking for dish photos at a table, the premise is weak and the next round does not run as planned.
  • Menu order. If two or more Round B participants read "No photo yet" as "not available" or "sold out", the placeholder wording changes before anything else.
  • Compare. If most participants decide between two dishes without opening Compare, it becomes a secondary action rather than a tab.
  • Source labels. If the label is noticed unprompted by fewer than half, it moves from the photo corner to the line beside the dish name.
  • Photo caveat. If Round C participants still read a photo as proof of ingredients after seeing the note, the note moves above the description and gains an example.

Each finding would be recorded with a count and the screen it changes. None of these sessions has been run.
