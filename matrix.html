This was not a strategy project. The scope and visual language were already set. My job was to resolve the interaction details, make a call, and give the developers screens they could build.

A VOTE SHOULD NOT
FEEL LIKE A FORM

The spec used two dropdowns to choose creators. I replaced them with tappable cards, so people could see faces and fan counts before voting. The change made it through review and into the handoff.

Two items in the lo-fi annotations did not make the final boards: swapping Market for Tournament during live events, and a per-screen language toggle. [PLACEHOLDER — VERIFY: The team deferred both to the next sprint so this handoff could stay focused on the core voting flow.]

DENSE MARKET DATA,
WITHOUT A TABLE

The market screens needed price, direction, and ownership status to scan quickly without looking like a spreadsheet.

  • Market: prices and trading for tokens and collectibles
  • Orders: filled and open in one list, one-tap cancel for open ones
  • Detail pages: per-creator token and NFT views, price history, buy/sell flows
  • Checkout: card or crypto, built to survive whichever processor the team picked
WHAT I COULD RESOLVE
IN ONE WEEK

These are the calls visible between the wireframes and final boards:

DecisionInstead ofWhy
Tappable creator cards for votingThe spec's two dropdown pickersCards make the vote a choice; fan counts give the tap a stake

Processor-agnostic checkout sheetDesigning to one payment providerThe team hadn't picked one; the layout survives either choice

The handoff included consistent components, spacing from the existing style guide, and the core happy paths. One week still left obvious gaps:

  • The boards show happy paths. [PLACEHOLDER — VERIFY: Loading, empty, and error states were not included in the handoff.]
  • Device chrome is inconsistent across two boards (iOS status bars on some screens, not others); I'd re-export before showing these again.
  • The neon-on-dark palette came with its contrast choices; small grey metadata on near-black is what I'd challenge first in a week two.

A second week would add the missing states and give the team a simple way to judge the handoff:

WHERE IT LANDED

The scope was fixed, but the details were not. Turning a dropdown into a set of creator cards was a small change that made the voting flow feel like the product it belonged to.
