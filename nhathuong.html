Nhat Huong — refreshing a 25-year-old brand without replacing it

Nhat Huong is a Vietnamese baking brand, established 1998, that now spans production, baker training, and distribution. By its own account it broke foreign brands' dominance of the domestic market. I was on the brand team for the refresh.

CONTEXT

Twenty-five years of product excellence, very little brand building. The refresh had one hard rule: the chef's-hat mark and the red were staying.

BRAND
STRATEGY

The team positioned the brand as an Inspirer: the story is the bakers it trains and supplies. The manifesto leads with national pride (Tự hào Thương hiệu Việt Nam, proudly a Vietnamese brand): real history, not a campaign line.

RETROSPECTIVE The archetypes available to a 25-year-old category leader:

ArchetypeThe story it tellsWhy it doesn't fit
Heritage Keeper: "since 1998, unchanged"Tradition, nostalgiaPoints backward; useless for the education arm and international ambitions
Modern Challenger: full reinventionNew brand, new marketThrows away the hat and the red, the two assets the client refused to spend
Inspirer (chosen)Pride pointed forwardPuts the legacy to work across production, education, and community

LOGO
REFRESH

The hat mark stays; proportions and typography were rebuilt: characters connect without collisions, and the wordmark follows the redrawn brim at any size.

COLOR &
TYPE

The heritage red stays at the center, joined by a vibrant pink. Eight named families with tint ramps give every product line a shelf identity inside one system:

  • Sweet Red & Candy Pink: the primary pair; heritage red, warmed up
  • Cream Yellow, Frost Mint, Milky Blue: the dairy-soft supporting range
  • Chocolate Brown, Blueberry Navy, Taro Purple: depth for premium lines
  • Type: a sturdy serif for heritage headlines, a clean sans for arm's-length reading — [ADD: name the typefaces, or note if custom]
GRAPHIC
SYSTEM

Five piped silhouettes, one per product group (cream, cake base, sauce, cheese, frozen), each turned into a repeat pattern. Their job is wayfinding: you should be able to tell the product group from across the aisle before you read a word. That's what the proposed shelf test below would check.

RETROSPECTIVE Component inventory, tallied from these boards:

  • Identity: refreshed mark + wordmark, 2 seal variants (Proudly Made in Vietnam · quality/est. seal), 3 lockup treatments (pink, red, black)
  • Color: 8 named families × 4-step tint ramps, hex + CMYK
  • Graphics: 5 product-group silhouettes → 5 repeat patterns
  • Packaging: cartons, tubs, squeeze bottles, frozen trays; bilingual label system with seals, usage icons, and barcodes
  • Applications: bag, fleet livery, cards, signage, OOH posters, social grid system

PROPOSED How I'd check whether it worked: show ~30 shoppers a shelf photo and time how fast they spot the brand in the new packaging versus the old (a photo isn't a store, and familiarity favors the old design, so read it carefully). Run a card sort with the logo hidden to see if people still group a product line as Nhat Huong; that's the pattern system's whole job. And watch distributor orders for the refreshed SKUs in season one, from client data I didn't have access to.

STATUS & WHAT'S TRUE
