Tenet — one deliberate moment

A trader writes rules for herself after the market closes. The next day, those rules step in when she tries to send an impulsive order. Everything runs inside two fictional brokers, Dart (mobile, 0DTE options) and Meridian (desktop, equities), with one shared Record underneath.

ROLEProduct design, interaction design, UX writing
SCOPE0→1 behavioral intervention system
STATUSFunctional interactive prototype · not shipped
TEAM · YEARIndependent · 2026
BUILDHTML/CSS/JavaScript prototype · AI‑assisted with Claude Design and Fable
The problemRetail apps push you to keep trading with streaks, confetti, and one-tap re-entry. Pro workstations do it with speed: hotkeys, dense ladders, sub-second tickets. Either way, after a few losses it's easy to send an order you haven't actually decided on.
The boundaryFriction, never a block. Tenet never sends an order on its own and never locks anyone out. You can always wait, reduce, edit, override, or cancel. Overrides just get logged; the Record doesn't editorialize about them.
StatusFunctional prototype · not shipped The rules, intervention state machines, cross-surface sync, and Record are fully functional. Brokers, quotes, fills, and history are simulated; no user study has been completed.

01 · THE
PROBLEM

On both kinds of interface, the next order can become automatic. That's my read, and it's untested. The design question: can a trader's own rule interrupt that momentum without turning into a lock, a punishment, or a trading signal?

02 · SCOPE &
ETHICAL BOUNDARY

What Tenet never does: send an order automatically, or lock a trader out.

  • What is simulated: Dart and Meridian are fictional; quotes, fills, and history are simulated and labeled. Broker integration and the import/analysis pipeline remain future work
  • What Tenet is not: a broker, a trading journal, a signal generator, financial advice, or a promise of profitability. One narrow job: restore a single reflective moment at send

03 · SYSTEM
MODEL

The whole system is one loop: write rules in the Companion after the close, the broker checks them at send, the intervention stays reversible, and the result lands in the Record. Three deliberate asymmetries hold it together:

Stricter → immediate.
Tightening syncs to both hosts right away; you can raise your bar mid-day.
Looser → next open.
Loosening waits until the next open. Otherwise you could talk yourself out of a rule at the exact moment it's supposed to stop you.
Failure → fail open.
If the connection drops, trading proceeds; the gap is recorded in plain view.

05 · THREE KEY
DESIGN DECISIONS

A · “Stepped in,” not “blocked.”
Early versions said “blocked.” That was wrong twice: nothing is actually blocked, and it makes the tool the author instead of the trader. The final copy is “Your rule stepped in.” The softer wording probably deters less, but deterrence was never the job. The trader deciding is.
B · Nothing sends by itself.
If the cooldown ended by sending the order, Tenet would be the one trading. So it ends in review instead, with reduce, wait, override, and cancel as equal options and none preselected. The extra confirmation costs real time in a fast market. I accepted that.
C · Evidence without false certainty.
“Tenet saved you $X” is a claim the system can't actually prove, so it never says it. Counts lead: how often rules stepped in, what happened next. Dollar figures come second, marked EST until they settle, with fees and slippage called out as excluded. Hedged numbers are worse marketing. I think they're better for trust.

06 · THE COMPANION —
RULES / PATTERNS / RECORD

In the after-close app, “What your rules did” leads with counts: 7× stepped in · 6× waited or trimmed · 1× continued past. Below them, estimated loss avoided $1,240 EST sits beside realized on overrides −$310 ACTUAL. Neither claims to be money earned, and both say what they are.

07 · TRUST, FAILURE &
ACCESSIBILITY

The parts a skeptical trader would ask about first:

  • Trust & data. Tenet reads order tickets at send and fills after close, conceptually via an embedded broker SDK, never your screen or other accounts. In the proposed model, disconnecting stops rules from arming and deleting the account removes rules and Record
  • Failure behavior. Failed sync surfaces an explicit retry. Connection loss fails open: trading proceeds and the gap shows in the Record (“feed lost 17 min · trading unaffected · logged”). Events are immutable; notes and error annotations attach alongside
  • Accessibility, as implemented in the prototypes: every intervention path is keyboard-complete; option groups use real radio semantics with arrow keys; status changes are announced live; charts carry text alternatives; reduced motion is respected. No WCAG certification is claimed

08 · ITERATION —
THREE HONEST CHANGES

Three things changed along the way:

  • A broad journal → one job. The first concept was a full trading journal with interventions bolted on: import, session analysis, annotation. I cut it down to just the intervention layer and shelved the rest
  • “Blocked” → a legible state machine. In early versions it wasn't clear whether the order was gone for good or whether the system might act on its own. The final flow spells it out: hold, then choose (reduce / wait / override / cancel), then re-confirm. Nothing happens automatically
  • Outcome-led evidence → behavior-led Record. The first evidence screen led with a big dollar figure, which implied Tenet makes you money. It doesn't, so that screen became the Record: computed live from what you actually do in the prototype, estimates marked EST, every fill labeled simulated

09 · LIMITATIONS &
VALIDATION PLAN

PROPOSED What this work cannot claim yet:

  • No moderated study yet; nothing here is validated with users
  • The broker SDK and import pipeline are conceptual; feasibility is argued, not proven
  • Market outcomes cannot validate behavioral effectiveness; a “saved” trade may have been a missed winner
  • Free-text override reasons and deeper evidence-to-fill linkage remain deferred

Planned first study: moderated think-aloud sessions on the working prototypes with 6–8 active options/futures traders, including prop-evaluation users. It would test comprehension of why a rule fired, estimate versus actual, stricter-immediate versus looser-at-open, tone at the intervention moment, and whether the design could inadvertently encourage more trading. A sample this size supports comprehension and tone findings, not efficacy claims.

10 · REFLECTION

The thing I'd defend hardest is that every path stays reversible. The product is really one moment: a pause that says why it happened and keeps every exit open. Next steps: run the study, and have a real feasibility conversation about the broker SDK.
