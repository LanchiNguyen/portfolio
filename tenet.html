TENET · Independent product concept · 2026

A moment to rethink

Tenet brings a trader’s own limits into the moment they place an order. If the order crosses a rule, the interface explains what stopped and what they can do next.

01 / OVERVIEW

Designing a clear
interruption.

The concept starts with a specific situation: an active trader has taken several losses and prepares a larger order. A rule written during a quieter moment is easy to forget when the next trade is one gesture away.

I focused on the moment the order is sent. The design has to explain the rule, handle overlapping limits, and make the next action unmistakable without taking over the trader’s decision.

What this project demonstrates

  • An end-to-end interaction model across mobile and desktop.
  • Explicit states for waiting, overriding, cancelling and reviewing.
  • Working rule changes and a shared decision record.

Independent concept. Brokers, prices and fills are simulated. No participant study or live broker integration has been completed. Tenet is not a broker, investment advice or a promise of profit.

02 / DESIGN DECISIONS

Make the consequence
visible before the choice.

Retrospective design rationale, documented in September 2026. These comparisons explain the current concept; they are not findings from user research.

01

Resolve overlapping rules together

A 12-contract order meets a 5-contract cap and a cooldown. “Reduce to 5 + wait 9 min” names both consequences in one action.

Tradeoff: a longer label, but less ambiguity than separate actions that leave one rule unresolved.

02

Review again after waiting

The quote can change during a pause. The order stays held until the trader reviews the refreshed quote and explicitly confirms or cancels.

Tradeoff: one extra step in a time-sensitive task. Finishing a timer never authorizes a trade.

03

Keep an override visible

The rule-following action leads visually. Override asks for a reason, then returns to a separate order review. Cancel remains a distinct exit.

Tradeoff: the hierarchy favors the earlier commitment. Testing must check whether people still notice and understand every exit.

One order, explicit states

  1. CheckCompare the order with the authored rules.
  2. ChooseReduce and wait, override with a reason, or cancel.
  3. ReviewInspect the current quote before confirming.
  4. RecordKeep the decision and its context.

Cancellation remains available during the wait and at review. An override skips the applicable pause, not the final confirmation.

04 / SYSTEM DESIGN

One rule model.
Different trading contexts.

Companion owns the rule

The trader can inspect the trigger and consequence before changing a rule. Lowering the options cap applies in Dart immediately. Raising it stays pending for the next market open; the current session keeps the stricter cap.

This protects the earlier commitment from an impulsive edit, but makes a legitimate mid-session change less flexible. Meridian’s separate equity cap remains 1,500 shares in this demo.

Explore rules and patterns →

Meridian keeps the workstation in view

Dart uses a prominent intervention inside the mobile ticket. Meridian puts the same decision beside a dense desktop workspace. The chart and positions remain visible; actions stay outside the scrolling explanation so the next step is always available.

Explore the Meridian workstation →

The Record shows behavior before money

A completed intervention records the rule, size and decision; an override includes the selected reason. Dart and Meridian events appear in Companion through storage shared in the same browser.

Counts answer “What did my rules do?” Dollar examples are secondary and simulated. A hypothetical loss avoided cannot establish that an intervention improved a trader’s decisions.

The prototype keeps original event entries when adding notes. This is a local demonstration, not a secure or tamper-proof trading ledger.

Rule behavior and failure boundaries
Loss cooldown + size cap
Resolve both rules together, override with a reason before waiting, or cancel.
Pacing
Wait or cancel, then review a refreshed quote. The timer never sends the order.
Wind-down
This hard limit reduces the allowance and stops new same-day-expiring options (0DTE) orders at 3:55 p.m., with no in-session override. Existing positions stay open. Unlike the cooldown, it is a boundary the trader commits to in advance.
Sync outage
The demo continues checking local rules and marks new events pending. It does not implement a fallback for failed broker-side enforcement.
05 / VALIDATION & NEXT STEPS

Working behavior is evidence.
Effectiveness still needs research.

What can be inspected now

The prototype implements combined-rule resolution, separate confirmation after waiting, cancellation, overrides, and local rule changes. These flows can be exercised directly and checked against their expected states.

Keyboard controls, named actions and status announcements support the core interactions. Comprehensive assistive-technology testing remains necessary.

What I would test next

A proposed moderated study with 6–8 active traders would focus on comprehension and control. It would not establish trading performance or behavioral efficacy.

  • Can they explain whether an order has been sent?
  • Do they understand both consequences of the combined action?
  • Can they find override and cancel without prompting?
  • Can they distinguish a pause, a hard limit and a pending rule change?

What would change the design

If someone believes the timer sends their order, the state language and review transition need revision before expanding the concept. If they miss cancel or override, the action hierarchy needs revision. A broker integration would also need an explicit enforcement-failure policy and a separate engineering feasibility review.

WHAT I TAKE FORWARD

Every interruption needs
a clear way forward.

Tenet let me carry one interaction problem across rule authoring, two broker interfaces, failure states and a reviewable Record. The most consequential design choice was separating waiting from permission to send.
